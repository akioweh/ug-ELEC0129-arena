## Question 1 — Describing the position of a rigid body [1 mark]

**Q.** How do you describe the position of a rigid body?

**Solution.** You will need:

- a reference frame (coordinate system)
- a frame rigidly attached to the rigid body B
- a vector from the origin of the reference frame to the origin of the frame which is rigidly attached to the rigid body B.

## Question 2 — Showing a rotation matrix is orthonormal [4 marks]

**Q.** Show that the rotation matrix

$${}^{A}_{B}R = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

is orthonormal.

**Solution.** We need to show that each column and each row has unit magnitude, and that each column and row is orthogonal (90 degrees) to the other columns and rows. *[1 mark]*

**Unit magnitudes:**

- First column: magnitude $= \sqrt{(\cos\theta)^2 + (\sin\theta)^2 + 0^2} = 1$
- Second column: magnitude $= \sqrt{(-\sin\theta)^2 + (\cos\theta)^2 + 0^2} = 1$
- Third column: magnitude $= \sqrt{0^2 + 0^2 + 1^2} = 1$
- First row: magnitude $= \sqrt{(\cos\theta)^2 + (-\sin\theta)^2 + 0^2} = 1$
- Second row: magnitude $= \sqrt{(\sin\theta)^2 + (\cos\theta)^2 + 0^2} = 1$
- Third row: magnitude $= \sqrt{0^2 + 0^2 + 1^2} = 1$

*[1.5 marks]*

**Orthogonality:**

- First & second column: $(\cos\theta)(-\sin\theta) + (\sin\theta)(\cos\theta) + (0)(0) = 0$
- First & third column: $(\cos\theta)(0) + (\sin\theta)(0) + (0)(1) = 0$
- Second & third column: $(-\sin\theta)(0) + (\cos\theta)(0) + (0)(1) = 0$
- First & second row: $(\cos\theta)(\sin\theta) + (-\sin\theta)(\cos\theta) + (0)(0) = 0$
- First & third row: $(\cos\theta)(0) + (-\sin\theta)(0) + (0)(1) = 0$
- Second & third row: $(\sin\theta)(0) + (\cos\theta)(0) + (0)(1) = 0$

*[1.5 marks]*

## Question 3 — Mapping a point between rotated frames [2.5 marks]

**Q.** Frame $\{B\}$ is obtained by rotating $\{A\}$ along the $z$-axis by 45 deg. In frame $\{B\}$, the point $P$ has a coordinate of $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^{T}$. What is the coordinate of point $P$ in frame $\{A\}$? Keep your answer in fraction instead of decimal points.

**Solution.**

First, get the description of $\{B\}$ in $\{A\}$:

$${}^{A}_{B}R = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & 0 \\ \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[1 mark]*

Then, calculate:

$${}^{A}P = {}^{A}_{B}R \cdot {}^{B}P = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & 0 \\ \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - 3\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 0 \\ 2\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 3\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 0 \\ 0 + 0 + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\ 5\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[1.5 marks]*

## Question 4 — Rewriting the mapping equation as a homogeneous transform [0.75 marks]

**Q.** Given ${}^{A}P = {}^{A}_{B}R \cdot {}^{B}P + {}^{A}P_{BORG}$, write the equation using homogeneous transform.

**Solution.**

$$\begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}P \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}_{B}R & {}^{A}P_{BORG} \\ 0\ 0\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} {}^{B}P \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[0.75 marks]*

## Question 5 — Derivation of the inverse of a homogeneous transform [2.5 marks]

**Q.** Given

$${}^{A}_{B}T = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}_{B}R & {}^{A}P_{BORG} \\ 0\ 0\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix},$$

derive the inverse of ${}^{A}_{B}T$. Note: you will need to show the derivation, not just give the final answer from your memory.

**Solution.**

- ${}^{A}P = {}^{A}_{B}T \cdot {}^{B}P$ gives ${}^{A}P$ in terms of ${}^{B}P$ $\to$ ${}^{A}P = {}^{A}_{B}R \cdot {}^{B}P + {}^{A}P_{BORG}$.
- If we can get ${}^{B}P$ in terms of ${}^{A}P$, then we would get the inverse of ${}^{A}_{B}T$.
- So, from the first line above:
  - ${}^{A}_{B}R \cdot {}^{B}P = {}^{A}P - {}^{A}P_{BORG}$ *[0.5 marks]*
  - ${}^{B}P = {}^{A}_{B}R^{-1} ({}^{A}P - {}^{A}P_{BORG}) = {}^{A}_{B}R^{-1} \cdot {}^{A}P - {}^{A}_{B}R^{-1} \cdot {}^{A}P_{BORG}$ *[0.5 marks]*
  - $\begin{bmatrix} {}^{B}P \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}_{B}R^{-1} & -{}^{A}_{B}R^{-1}\,{}^{A}P_{BORG} \\ 0\ 0\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}P \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ *[1 mark]*
- Thus the inverse of ${}^{A}_{B}T$ is:
  - ${}^{A}_{B}T^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}_{B}R^{-1} & -{}^{A}_{B}R^{-1}\,{}^{A}P_{BORG} \\ 0\ 0\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
- But we also know that the inverse of a rotation matrix is the same as its transpose, thus:
  - ${}^{A}_{B}T^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{A}_{B}R^{T} & -{}^{A}_{B}R^{T}\,{}^{A}P_{BORG} \\ 0\ 0\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ *[0.5 marks]*

## Question 6 — Extracting ZYX (or Z-Y-X Euler) angles from a rotation matrix [3 marks]

**Q.** Given

$${}^{A}_{B}R = \begin{bmatrix} r_{11} & r_{12} & r_{13} \\ r_{21} & r_{22} & r_{23} \\ r_{31} & r_{32} & r_{33} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} c\alpha c\beta & c\alpha s\beta s\gamma - s\alpha c\gamma & c\alpha s\beta c\gamma + s\alpha s\gamma \\ s\alpha c\beta & s\alpha s\beta s\gamma + c\alpha c\gamma & s\alpha s\beta c\gamma - c\alpha s\gamma \\ -s\beta & c\beta s\gamma & c\beta c\gamma \end{bmatrix}$$

Calculate $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Specify also the condition when some answers cannot be obtained. (Note: $c\alpha$ is the shorthand notation for $\cos\alpha$, $s\alpha$ is the shorthand notation for $\sin\alpha$. Similar abbreviation for $\beta$ and $\gamma$.)

**Solution.** (Note: there can be different ways of solving the question.)

- First, we notice that $-s\beta = r_{31}$ or $s\beta = -r_{31}$.
- Thus $c\beta = \pm\sqrt{1 - s^2\beta} = \pm\sqrt{1 - r_{31}^2}$.
- So $\beta = \mathrm{atan2}\!\left(-r_{31},\ \pm\sqrt{1 - r_{31}^2}\right)$. *[1 mark]*
- Next, we notice that $c\beta\, s\gamma = r_{32}$ and $c\beta\, c\gamma = r_{33}$.
- Thus $s\gamma = \dfrac{r_{32}}{c\beta}$ and $c\gamma = \dfrac{r_{33}}{c\beta}$.
- So $\gamma = \mathrm{atan2}\!\left(\dfrac{r_{32}}{c\beta},\ \dfrac{r_{33}}{c\beta}\right)$. *[1 mark]*
- Finally, we notice that $c\alpha\, c\beta = r_{11}$ and $s\alpha\, c\beta = r_{21}$.
- Thus $c\alpha = \dfrac{r_{11}}{c\beta}$ and $s\alpha = \dfrac{r_{21}}{c\beta}$.
- So $\alpha = \mathrm{atan2}\!\left(\dfrac{r_{21}}{c\beta},\ \dfrac{r_{11}}{c\beta}\right)$. *[0.5 marks]*
- There is no solution when $c\beta = 0$, or $\beta = \pm 90^{\circ}$. *[0.5 marks]*

**Q.** How many parameters are there in DH Parameters? And what are they (full names and symbols)?

**Solution.** Four parameters. They are the link lengths $a$, link twists $\alpha$, link offsets $d$ and joint angles $\theta$. *[0.75 marks]*

## Question 10 — General idea of forward kinematics via systematic DH [3 marks]

**Q.** In your own words, explain the general idea of solving the forward kinematics problem using the systematic method of DH parameters.

**Solution.**

First we draw axes, and then we determine the mutual perpendiculars between axes. Frames will be attached at each joint, with the $z$-axis pointing along the axis, and the $x$-axis pointing along the mutual perpendiculars. *[1 mark]*

With the frames fixed, we can then determine the DH parameters, followed by calculation of the transformation matrices from lower axis to higher axis. At the end, we calculate the compound transformation, and we will get the transformation matrix from base to the last joint. *[1 mark]*

The end-effector position is fixed with respect to the last joint, and we can then calculate the end-effector position with respect to the base using the mapping equation. *[1 mark]*

## Question 12 — Forward kinematics from a numerical DH table [23 marks]

**Q.** A DH table is as follows (Table 12.1):

**Table 12.1 — DH Table**

| $i$ | $a_{i-1}$ | $\alpha_{i-1}$ | $d_i$ | $\theta_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $3$ | $30^{\circ}$ | $0$ | $\theta_1$ |
| 2 | $2$ | $0^{\circ}$ | $d_2$ | $30^{\circ}$ |
| 3 | $1$ | $60^{\circ}$ | $1$ | $\theta_3$ |

The transformation matrix from frame $i-1$ to frame $i$ is given by:

$${}^{i-1}_{i}T = \begin{bmatrix} c\theta_i & -s\theta_i & 0 & a_{i-1} \\ c\alpha_{i-1}s\theta_i & c\alpha_{i-1}c\theta_i & -s\alpha_{i-1} & -s\alpha_{i-1}d_i \\ s\alpha_{i-1}s\theta_i & s\alpha_{i-1}c\theta_i & c\alpha_{i-1} & c\alpha_{i-1}d_i \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[23 marks]*

**Solution.**

For first row ($i=1$): $s\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2},\ c\alpha = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Thus

$${}^{0}_{1}T = \begin{bmatrix} c\theta_1 & -s\theta_1 & 0 & 3 \\ \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s\theta_1 & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}c\theta_1 & -\tfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\ \tfrac{1}{2}s\theta_1 & \tfrac{1}{2}c\theta_1 & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[4 marks]*

For second row ($i=2$): $s\alpha = 0,\ c\alpha = 1,\ s\theta = \tfrac{1}{2},\ c\theta = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Thus

$${}^{1}_{2}T = \begin{bmatrix} \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\tfrac{1}{2} & 0 & 2 \\ \tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & d_2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[4 marks]*

For third row ($i=3$): $c\alpha = \tfrac{1}{2},\ s\alpha = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Thus

$${}^{2}_{3}T = \begin{bmatrix} c\theta_3 & -s\theta_3 & 0 & 1 \\ \tfrac{1}{2}s\theta_3 & \tfrac{1}{2}c\theta_3 & -\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\ \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s\theta_3 & \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}c\theta_3 & \tfrac{1}{2} & \tfrac{1}{2} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

*[4 marks]*

**Compound transformation:**

$${}^{0}_{3}T = {}^{0}_{1}T \cdot {}^{1}_{2}T \cdot {}^{2}_{3}T$$

Multiplying ${}^{0}_{1}T \cdot {}^{1}_{2}T$ gives an intermediate $4\times 4$ matrix:

$${}^{0}_{1}T \cdot {}^{1}_{2}T = \begin{bmatrix}

## Question 16 — Geometric inverse kinematics for an RRR arm [6 marks]

**Q.** Given a robot as shown in Fig 16.1, derive the inverse kinematics of the robot if the end-effector is required to be at ${}^{0}P = [p_x\ p_y\ p_z]^{T}$ using the geometric approach.

**Figure 16.1 — Schematics of an RRR Robot.** The robot is a 3R serial chain. The base is at the bottom, attached to ground (hatched ground symbol). The first joint $\theta_1$ has its axis along $z_0$ (vertical, pointing up), with base frame $\{x_0, y_0, z_0\}$ shown at the shoulder. Link $L_2$ then extends out and slightly upward at angle $\theta_3$ at the next revolute joint, terminating at point $P$. ## Question 18 — Linear Jacobian and singularities of an end-effector [13 marks]

**Q.** The end-effector has the following position vector with respect to frame $\{0\}$:

$${}^{0}P = \begin{bmatrix} L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + L_2 \cos\theta_2 \\ d_3 \\ L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + d_3 \sin\theta_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

where $L_i$'s are constants, while $\theta_i$'s and $d_i$'s are variables. Calculate the linear Jacobian matrix of the end-effector with respect to frame $\{0\}$. Does the end-effector have any singularities? If yes, please specify when singularities would happen.

*[13 marks]*

**Solution.**

$$x = L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + L_2 \cos\theta_2$$
$$y = d_3$$
$$z = L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) + d_3 \sin\theta_2$$

$$J = \begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial x}{\partial \theta_1} & \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial \theta_2} & \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial d_3} \\[2mm]
\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial \theta_1} & \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial \theta_2} & \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial d_3} \\[2mm]
\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial \theta_1} & \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial \theta_2} & \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial d_3}
\end{bmatrix}$$

$$= \begin{bmatrix}
L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 \sin\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) & L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_2 \sin\theta_2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1 \\
-L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 \sin\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) & -L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + d_3 \cos\theta_2 & \sin\theta_2
\end{bmatrix}$$

*[4 marks]*

To find singularity, we need to find the determinant of $J$ and set it to zero. Expanding along row 2 (which has only the entry $1$ in column 3), the $3\times 3$ determinant reduces to the $2\times 2$ minor formed from rows 1 and 3, columns 1 and 2:

$$\det(J) = \begin{vmatrix}
L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 \sin\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) & L_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_2 \sin\theta_2 \\
-L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 \sin\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) & -L_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + d_3 \cos\theta_2
\end{vmatrix}$$

Expanding:

$$\det(J) = -L_1^2 \cos^2\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + L_1 d_3 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \cos\theta_2$$
$$\quad + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1 \sin^2(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 d_3 \sin\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) \cos\theta_2$$
$$\quad + L_1^2 \cos^2\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 L_2 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) \sin\theta_2$$
$$\quad + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1 \cos^2(\theta_1+\theta_2) - L_1 L_2 \sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$$
$$= L_1 d_3 \cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \cos\theta_2$$
$$\quad + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1 - L_1 d_3 \sin\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) \cos\theta_2$$
$$\quad - L_1 L_2 \cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) \sin\theta_2$$
$$\quad - L_1 L_2 \sin\theta_1 \sin\theta_2 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$$
$$= L_1 d_3 \cos\theta_2 \big(\cos\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) - \sin\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\big) + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1$$
$$\quad - L_1 L_2 \sin\theta_2 \big(\cos\theta_1 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + \sin\theta_1 \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)\big)$$
$$= L_1 d_3 \cos\theta_2 \cos(2\theta_1+\theta_2) + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1 - L_1 L_2 \sin\theta_2 \sin(2\theta_1+\theta_2)$$

*[6 marks]*

When $d_3 = L_2$, we have:

$$\det(J) = L_1 L_2 \cos\theta_2 \cos(2\theta_1+\theta_2) + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1 - L_1 L_2 \sin\theta_2 \sin(2\theta_1+\theta_2)$$
$$= L_1 L_2 \cos(2\theta_1 + 2\theta_2) + L_1^2 \sin\theta_1 \cos\theta_1$$

Possible singularities when:

- $\theta_1 = 0^{\circ}$, $\theta_2 = 45^{\circ}$, $d_3 = L_2$
- $\theta_1 = 90^{\circ}$, $\theta_2 = 45^{\circ}$, $d_3 = L_2$

*[3 marks]*

## Question 20 — Cubic-polynomial trajectory parameters [5.5 marks]

**Q.** Explain the procedure to calculate the parameters $a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3$ for a cubic polynomial $u(t) = a_0 + a_1 t + a_2 t^2 + a_3 t^3$, where the starting position is $u_0$ and the final position is $u_f$. The time to reach the final position is $t_f$.

**Solution.**

- Set $u(t=0) = a_0 = u_0$ and we will get $a_0$. *[1 mark]*
- Differentiate the cubic polynomial to get the velocity equation: $\dot{u}(t) = a_1 + 2 a_2 t + 3 a_3 t^2$.
- Set $\dot{u}(t=0) = a_1 = 0$ and we will get $a_1$. *[1 mark]*
- To get $a_2$ and $a_3$ we will need to solve the following simultaneous equations:
  - $u(t = t_f) = a_0 + a_1 t_f + a_2 t_f^2 + a_3 t_f^3 = u_f$
  - $\dot{u}(t = t_f) = a_1 + 2 a_2 t_f + 3 a_3 t_f^2 = 0$ *[1.5 marks]*
- This gives:
  - $\begin{bmatrix} t_f^2 & t_f^3 \\ 2 t_f & 3 t_f^2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a_2 \\ a_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} u_f - u_0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$
  - $\begin{bmatrix} a_2 \\ a_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{3}{t_f^2}(u_f - u_0) \\[1mm] -\dfrac{2}{t_f^3}(u_f - u_0) \end{bmatrix}$ *[2 marks]*

## Question 21 — Position/velocity/acceleration profiles for a quintic [0.75 marks]

**Q.** Sketch the position, velocity and acceleration profiles for a quintic polynomial. Assume $u_0$ is less than $u_f$.

**Solution.** Three plots side by side over a common time interval (e.g. $t \in [0, 3]$):

- **Position.** A smooth, monotonically increasing S-shaped curve from $u_0$ at $t=0$ to $u_f$ at $t=t_f$. Tangent is horizontal at both endpoints (zero start/finish velocity).
- **Velocity.** A smooth bell-shape (single hump) starting at $0$, rising to a positive peak around mid-interval, and returning to $0$ at $t_f$. Tangent is horizontal at both endpoints (zero start/finish acceleration).
- **Acceleration.** Starts at $0$, rises to a positive peak in the first third, crosses zero around mid-interval, falls to a symmetric negative trough in the last third, and returns to $0$ at $t_f$ — i.e. an "N-shape" / one-period-of-a-sinusoid-like curve.

*[0.75 marks]*

## Question 22 — General idea of Newton-Euler dynamics [2.5 marks]

**Q.** Explain the general idea of calculating the dynamic equation of robots using the Newton-Euler Formula.

**Solution.**

- Propagate velocity and acceleration from lower frame to upper frame.
- Propagate the acceleration of each frame to the centre of mass of each link.
- With acceleration of centre of mass, and the mass of link, we can calculate the force on the link via $F = ma$.
- Propagate joint forces (external force, motor torques) from higher frame to lower frame.
- The force on link, $F$, can then be related to the joint forces, and these will give dynamic equations.

*[2.5 marks]*

## Question 25 — Mass moment of inertia $I_{xx}$ of a uniform rectangular block [5 marks]

**Q.** Given a rectangular block with length $l$, width $w$ and height $h$ as shown in Fig 25.1, calculate the mass moment of inertia $I_{xx}$. Assume the volume is $V$, the density $\rho$ is uniform and the mass is $m$.

## Question 27 — Free response of a mass-spring-damper [5 marks]

**Q.** Determine the motion of a mass-spring-damper system if the parameter values are $m=3$, $b=2$, $k=4$, and the mass (initially at rest) is released from position $x=2$.

**Solution.** The system is:

$$m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0$$

And with values:

$$3\ddot{x} + 2\dot{x} + 4x = 0$$

The characteristic equation is:

$$3\lambda^2 + 2\lambda + 4 = 0$$

The roots of the equation are:

$$\lambda_{1,2} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{2^2 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}}{2 \cdot 3} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{-44}}{6} = -\tfrac{1}{3} \pm j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{44}}{6} = -\tfrac{1}{3} \pm j\,\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3}$$

The general solution for $x$ is therefore:

$$x(t) = c_1\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right) + c_2\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right)$$

*[2 marks]*

The velocity equation is:

$$\dot{x}(t) = -\tfrac{1}{3} c_1\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right) - c_1\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right) - \tfrac{1}{3} c_2\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right) + c_2\, e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right)$$

<!-- suspected-source-error: the printed velocity expression omits the multiplicative factor √11/3 in front of the sine/cosine terms generated by differentiating the trigonometric arguments. The boundary-condition substitution below proceeds as if these factors are absorbed into c₂, so the final answer is internally consistent but the displayed derivative is incomplete. Transcribed verbatim. -->

To calculate $c_1$ and $c_2$, put in the boundary conditions:

$$x(t=0) = c_1 = 2$$
$$\dot{x}(t=0) = -\tfrac{1}{3} c_1 + c_2 = 0$$
$$c_2 = \tfrac{1}{3} c_1 = \tfrac{2}{3}$$

So the equation is:

$$x(t) = 2 e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \cos\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right) + \tfrac{2}{3} e^{-\tfrac{1}{3} t} \sin\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{11}}{3} t\right)$$
